HMRC nudge letters and letters to online sellers: what to do

A 'nudge' letter is HMRC asking you to check your tax, because it has information that you may have income you haven't told it about – often from selling online. It is not a fine and not an investigation, but don't ignore it.

Why HMRC wrote to you

Since 1 January 2024, websites and apps such as eBay, Vinted, Etsy and Airbnb have had to collect details of their sellers and report them to HMRC each January. HMRC compares this with tax returns and writes to people whose income doesn't seem to have been declared.

Your details are reported if you make 30 or more sales, or receive about £1,700 (€2,000) or more, in a calendar year. Being reported doesn't mean you owe tax.

HMRC sends similar letters about other income too, for example rental income, overseas income or crypto, and when it expected a tax return that didn't arrive.

Is your online selling taxable?

  • Selling your own unwanted things – clothes, the contents of your loft – for less than you paid is usually not trading, and usually not taxable.
  • Buying or making things to sell at a profit is likely to be trading, and the profit is taxable.
  • If your trading income is £1,000 or less in a tax year, the trading allowance usually means you don't need to tell HMRC about it.

Renting out a room or a property (Airbnb, for example) follows its own rules, including a separate £1,000 property allowance and the Rent a Room scheme.

What to do now

  1. Note the date HMRC asks you to reply by.
  2. Work out what you sold, and whether it was your own belongings or trading. Platforms send you a copy of what they reported.
  3. If you owe tax, tell HMRC and file the returns. Owning up before HMRC asks again usually means lower penalties.
  4. If you don't owe anything, reply to say so – keep your records in case HMRC asks.

If the letter mentions a compliance check, or asks for documents within a set time, it is more than a nudge – speak to an accountant.

Questions

Do I have to pay tax on things I sold on Vinted or eBay?

Usually not, if you were selling your own used belongings. Buying or making things to sell for a profit is trading, and profit above the £1,000 trading allowance is taxable.

Is a nudge letter an investigation?

No. It asks you to check your tax and correct it if needed. If you ignore it, HMRC may open a formal check later.

I was reported by a platform. Does that mean I owe tax?

No. Platforms report everyone above the threshold. Whether you owe tax depends on what you sold and whether it was trading.
