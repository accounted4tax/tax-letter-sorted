HMRC daily penalties and 6 and 12-month late filing penalties

If your Self Assessment return is more than 3 months late, HMRC adds more penalties on top of the £100: £10 a day, then 5% or £300 at 6 months, and again at 12 months.

How the penalties build up

For an online return due on 31 January, the timeline looks like this if the return is still not filed:

Return late byPenaltyRunning total (at least)
1 day£100£100
3 months£10 a day for up to 90 days£1,000
6 months5% of the tax due or £300£1,300
12 monthsAnother 5% of the tax due or £300£1,600

The daily penalties stop once 90 days have been charged. Late payment penalties and interest are separate, on top of these.

If your return still isn't filed

For the 6-month and 12-month penalties, HMRC works out the tax it thinks you owe and bases the 5% on that estimate. Filing the return replaces the estimate with your real figures, which can lower these penalties – and it stops the 12-month penalty if you file before then.

At 12 months the penalty can be much higher than 5% if HMRC believes information was deliberately held back. That needs an accountant, not a self-help appeal.

Check the letter

  • Dates: if you filed less than 3 months after the deadline, you shouldn't have daily penalties. If you have them anyway, HMRC may have made a mistake.
  • Amount: the 6 and 12-month penalties should be £300, or 5% of the tax due if that is higher. A much bigger figure needs checking.
  • Same tax year: all the late filing penalties for one tax year – including the £100 – can go on one SA370, which has a section for each penalty.

Can you appeal?

The test is the same as for the £100 penalty: a reasonable excuse that lasted through the period and stopped you filing, and filing as soon as you could afterwards. For long delays, HMRC looks closely at the whole period – a short illness won't explain a return that was 9 months late.

Examples HMRC gives include a death in the family shortly before the deadline, an unexpected hospital stay or serious illness, failure of your computer or of HMRC's online services, and fire, flood or theft. See the full list on GOV.UK.

How to appeal

  1. File the return if it isn't filed yet.
  2. Appeal within 30 days of the date on each penalty notice (later appeals need a reason for the delay).
  3. Appeal online, or post form SA370 to: Self Assessment, HM Revenue and Customs, BX9 1AS.
  4. Give dates for when the problem started and ended, and say what evidence you have.

Questions

When do HMRC daily penalties start?

Once the return is 3 months late. For a return due on 31 January, that is from 1 May. They run at £10 a day for up to 90 days.

Can I appeal the £100 and the daily penalties together?

Yes. Penalties for the same tax year can go on one SA370 form, with a section for each penalty.

Why is my 6-month penalty more than £300?

It is 5% of the tax due if that is more than £300. If you haven't filed, HMRC uses its own estimate of the tax – filing your return replaces it.

Does filing my return now stop the penalties?

It stops further late filing penalties from being added, and replaces HMRC's estimate. It doesn't cancel penalties already charged – for that you need to appeal.
