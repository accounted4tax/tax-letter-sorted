HMRC Making Tax Digital for Income Tax letter: what you need to do

Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax means sole traders and landlords keep digital records and send HMRC an update every quarter, then a tax return after the year ends. HMRC writes to people who have to start.

Why HMRC wrote to you

HMRC looks at your Self Assessment return to see whether your qualifying income – your self-employment turnover plus your property income, before expenses – is over the limit. If it is, you have to use MTD from the date shown. Partnerships aren't included yet.

If you should have started on 6 April 2026 and haven't signed up yet, you can still sign up now.

What changes

  • Keep your business and property records in MTD-compatible software.
  • Send a quarterly update for each business and property: by 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May.
  • Send your tax return through the software after the year ends, by 31 January.
  • You still pay tax on the usual Self Assessment dates: 31 January and 31 July.

Penalties

Late quarterly updates come under a points system. For the 2026-27 tax year HMRC won't give penalty points for late quarterly updates – but the updates and digital records are still required, and a late tax return still gets points. Don't rely on the soft landing as a reason to wait.

Exemptions

Some people are exempt, for example if they are digitally excluded because of age, disability, location or other reasons. Some exemptions are automatic; others you have to apply for. Exempt people still file a Self Assessment return.

What to do now

  1. Check the start date in your letter.
  2. Choose MTD-compatible software, or ask an accountant to do it for you.
  3. Sign up before your first quarter ends, and keep digital records from the start date.

Questions

Do I have to use Making Tax Digital?

Only if your qualifying income – self-employment plus property income, before expenses – is over the limit: £50,000 in 2024-25, £30,000 in 2025-26 or £20,000 in 2026-27. HMRC writes to people who have to start.

When are MTD quarterly updates due?

By 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May for the standard quarters of the tax year.

Will I get a penalty for a late quarterly update in 2026-27?

HMRC has said it won't give penalty points for late quarterly updates in 2026-27. The updates are still required, and a late tax return still gets penalty points.
