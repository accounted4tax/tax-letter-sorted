PAYE late filing penalty for employers: what it means

HMRC charges employers a penalty when payroll reports – the Full Payment Submission (FPS) – reach HMRC late, or when a month with no payments isn't reported. The amount depends on how many employees you have.

Why you got the penalty

Under Real Time Information (RTI), an employer must send an FPS to HMRC every time employees are paid, on or before payday. If a report is late, or a month with no pay isn't reported, HMRC can charge a penalty for each month. Employers with more than one PAYE scheme can be charged for each one.

When there shouldn't be a penalty

  • Within 3 days: the FPS was late, but all the payments on it were made within 3 days of payday.
  • New employer: your first FPS was sent within 30 days of first paying an employee.
  • First time this tax year: HMRC doesn't charge for the first late report in a tax year (this doesn't apply to annual schemes).

If one of these fits, the penalty may be wrong and can be appealed.

Estimated charges

If reports are missing, HMRC may also send an estimated bill for the PAYE it thinks is due. Only sending the missing reports replaces it with the real figures.

How to appeal

You can appeal if the penalty isn't due, the amount is wrong, or you had a reasonable excuse.

  1. Appeal within 30 days of the penalty notice.
  2. Online: sign in to HMRC's PAYE for employers service and choose Appeal a penalty. Take a screenshot of the confirmation – it can't be printed.
  3. By post: National Insurance Contributions and Employers Office, HM Revenue and Customs, BX9 1BX.
  4. Quote the unique ID of each penalty from the notice.

Questions

How much is the PAYE late filing penalty?

£100 a month for 1 to 9 employees, £200 for 10 to 49, £300 for 50 to 249 and £400 for 250 or more.

Is there a penalty if the FPS is only a day or two late?

Usually not, if all the payments on it were made within 3 days of payday. HMRC may still contact employers who are often late within those 3 days.

Do I get a penalty the first time I'm late?

No. The first late report in a tax year isn't charged, unless you're registered as an annual scheme.
