HMRC £100 late filing penalty: what it means and how to appeal

HMRC charges a fixed £100 penalty when your Self Assessment tax return arrives after the deadline. It applies even if you owe no tax, or paid your tax on time.

Why you got this letter

The letter (often form SA326D) means HMRC did not receive your tax return by the deadline. For an online return that is 31 January after the end of the tax year, so a 2024-25 return was due by 31 January 2026.

The £100 is charged whether or not you owe tax. If you had no income to report, it is still charged unless HMRC agrees you didn't need to send a return.

Check it before you pay

  • You filed on time? Find your online submission receipt. If it shows the return arrived before the deadline, HMRC may have made a mistake and you can appeal with that proof.
  • HMRC asked you late? If HMRC sent the notice to file after 31 October, your deadline is 3 months from the date of that notice, not 31 January.
  • You didn't need a return? If you shouldn't have been in Self Assessment for that year, ask HMRC to cancel the return and the penalty.

What happens if you do nothing

The £100 is only the start. If the return is still not filed:

Can you appeal?

HMRC cancels the penalty if you had a reasonable excuse: something unexpected or outside your control stopped you filing on time, and you filed as soon as you could once it was over.

Examples HMRC gives include:

  • a partner or close relative died shortly before the deadline
  • an unexpected stay in hospital, or a serious or life-threatening illness
  • your computer or software failed while you were preparing the return
  • HMRC's online services were not working
  • a fire, flood or theft stopped you completing the return

These usually don't count: finding the online system too hard, not getting a reminder from HMRC, or not being able to pay. HMRC's full list is on GOV.UK.

Your appeal has to be true and based on what actually happened. We never suggest a reason for you – we only use the facts you confirm.

How to appeal

  1. File the return first if you haven't already.
  2. Appeal within 30 days of the date on the penalty notice. A later appeal can still be accepted if you explain why it is late.
  3. Choose how to send it: online through HMRC's appeal service, or on form SA370 by post to: Self Assessment, HM Revenue and Customs, BX9 1AS.
  4. Include dates and evidence: when the problem started and ended, when you filed, and anything that backs it up (for example a hospital letter).

You don't have to pay the penalty while HMRC looks at your appeal. If the appeal is refused, you'll owe interest from the original due date. If you disagree with HMRC's decision, you can ask for a review or go to the tax tribunal.

Questions

Do I have to pay the £100 if I owe no tax?

Yes. The penalty is for sending the return late, not for paying late, so it applies even if no tax is due. If you didn't need to send a return at all, you can ask HMRC to cancel it.

Can I appeal after 30 days?

Yes. HMRC can accept a late appeal if you explain why you couldn't appeal on time, for example because you were in hospital.

I filed on time but still got the penalty. What should I do?

Keep your online submission receipt and appeal, saying the return was received on time. HMRC should cancel the penalty if its records are wrong.

Is being too busy or forgetting a reasonable excuse?

Usually not. HMRC looks for something unexpected or outside your control that stopped you filing, and expects you to file as soon as the problem was over.
